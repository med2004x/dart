# Step 08: University Mathematics

This is a full university-level mathematics requirement. Do not treat it as optional “math for programmers”. The target is real competence in analysis/calculus, linear algebra, discrete mathematics, probability, and statistics, followed by engineering applications.

## Order

1. `step-01-analysis` — single-variable calculus, sequences/series, multivariable calculus
2. `step-02-linear-algebra` — vectors, matrices, linear systems, eigenvalues, orthogonality, least squares
3. `step-03-discrete-math` — proofs, logic, sets, combinatorics, graph theory, recurrences, asymptotics
4. `step-04-probability` — probability spaces, conditional probability, random variables, distributions, limit theorems
5. `step-05-statistics` — descriptive statistics, estimation, confidence intervals, testing, regression, experimental design
6. `step-06-math-for-engineering` — queueing, capacity, reliability, and system modeling

## University references

MIT OpenCourseWare 18.01SC covers single-variable calculus with lectures, recitations, worked examples, problem sets, and exams.
MIT 18.06 covers linear algebra with lectures, notes, problem sets, and exams.
MIT 18.05 covers probability and statistics with problem sets, exams, Bayesian inference, hypothesis testing, confidence intervals, and regression.
MIT 6.042J covers discrete mathematics for computer science, including proofs, graph theory, counting, recurrences, asymptotics, and discrete probability.

Use the exact course links in `../../resources/EXTERNAL-REPOS.md`.

## Completion standard

For each lesson, solve problems by hand first. Then verify selected results computationally. Keep a written record of mistakes and corrections. A calculator producing a correct number is not a proof of understanding.
